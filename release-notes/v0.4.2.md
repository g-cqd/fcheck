# fcheck 0.4.2 (from 0.4.1)

fcheck reports fewer shapes that are not bugs, and reads two more ways of replacing a file. The pins are those of 0.4.1:
analyzerkit `d76118d`, AemiJSON `cbac433`, AemiProtobuf `7393728`, aemi `0ef4279`. On the g-cqd corpora the findings went from
ask 15 → 10, Atelier 5 → 1, analyzerkit 1 → 0; aemi (3) and TerminalKit (2) are unchanged. On the reference app it is the same two:
the cut-then-decode in `Translator.swift` and the leak-on-exit in `ScannerActor.swift`.

## Findings that may appear

- `replace-not-durable` for `FileManager.replaceItemAt(_:withItemAt:…)` and the older `replaceItem(at:withItemAt:…)`, which are
  replacing renames. A method of the program's own with another label (`replaceItem(withId:)`) is not one.
- A handle that is acquired and stored by a bare assignment to a name the function does not declare (a member or a global) is
  recognised as stored, so it is no longer reported; the reverse, a handle acquired and never let go, is reported as before.

## Findings that may disappear

- `leak-on-exit` for `fopen` and `opendir` on the path where the result is nil, as for a descriptor that is `-1`.
- `leak-on-exit` for `device?.lockForConfiguration()` and `device!.…` pairs, which are now followed by the receiver's name.
- `leak-on-exit` for an object returned by a function of the corpus whose own method lets go of it (`lock.release()`): a method
  called on a held handle means an object that lets go of itself.
- `cut-then-decode` where the slice starts or stops at a variable that a `firstIndex`, `lastIndex` or `range` search gave (an
  ASCII delimiter, which no character straddles).
- `cut-then-decode` where `dropFirst(X.count)` or `prefix(X.count)` follows `starts(with: X)`, `hasPrefix` or `hasSuffix`: the
  marker that was matched is whole characters.
- `cut-then-decode` where the count is the length that `read`, `pread`, `recv`, `fread`, `readlink`, `proc_pidpath` or `getcwd`
  returned: the bytes end there, they were not cut.
